Why maintenance matters more in the desert

Dust and long idle periods do more damage here than heavy use does. A system sits untouched for months, then gets asked for everything at once.

In El Paso an air conditioner works hard from May to September and a furnace works hard for about six weeks. The rest of the year both sit still, collecting dust. That pattern is exactly what wears out capacitors, clogs coils and dries out seals, and it is why the failures cluster on the first genuinely hot day and the first genuinely cold night.

Most breakdowns we are called out to were preventable. A tune-up finds the worn capacitor or the low charge before it strands you.

What a tune-up covers

  • Refrigerant charge checked and measured, not guessed
  • Coils inspected and cleaned
  • Electrical connections checked and tightened
  • Capacitors and contactors tested before they fail
  • Condensate drain cleared
  • Thermostat calibration checked
  • Airflow and temperature split measured
  • Evaporative systems: pads, float, pump and bleed-off checked

Two visits a year, timed for this climate

  1. 1

    Spring cooling check

    Before the first hot week, so the system is ready when you need it rather than being tested by July.

  2. 2

    Autumn heating check

    Ideally in early October. This visit helps find heating faults before the first cold spell.

Maintenance questions we get asked

How often should an air conditioner be serviced in El Paso?

Twice a year: once before the cooling season and once before the heating season. In a desert climate dust and long idle periods do more damage than heavy use, so an annual-only schedule tends to miss what the off-season left behind.

Does a maintenance plan cover repairs as well as tune-ups?

The plan covers the scheduled tune-up visits and the inspection work described above. If a visit finds a fault that needs a repair, we explain what we found and what it involves, and you decide before any work starts.

Do you maintain evaporative coolers as well as refrigerated air?

Yes. Whether you have a refrigerated or an evaporative system, the maintenance club covers it, including pads, pump, float and bleed-off on evaporative units.
