Should I repair or replace an old air conditioner?

Look at four things: how old the system is, what refrigerant it uses, how many repairs it has needed recently, and what this particular repair involves. A system under ten years old with its first significant fault is usually worth repairing. A system past fifteen that runs on R-22 refrigerant and has needed work two summers running is usually worth replacing. Most decisions are clearer than people expect once those four are on the table.

The four things that decide it

  1. 1

    Age against expected life

    Air conditioners here work harder than the national average because of the run hours. A system in its mid teens has done a lot of work. That does not mean it must be replaced, but it changes how much you should spend on it.

  2. 2

    What refrigerant it uses

    Systems built before roughly 2010 often use R-22, which has been phased out of production in the United States. It is still legal to service equipment with reclaimed supply, but it is scarce and the price reflects that. A large R-22 repair on an old system is usually the point where replacement makes more sense.

  3. 3

    How often it has needed work

    One repair is an event. Three repairs across two summers is a pattern, and the pattern usually continues. Compressors, coils and control boards tend to reach the end of their lives at around the same time.

  4. 4

    What this repair actually is

    A capacitor or a contactor is a normal wear part on any system at any age. A compressor or an evaporator coil is a major component, and on an old system that is a different conversation.

The rule of thumb people quote, and its limits

You will find a widely repeated rule online: multiply the age of the system by the cost of the repair, and if the result is more than five thousand, replace it. It is a reasonable way to force a decision, and it captures something real, which is that money spent on an old system buys you less remaining life than the same money on a newer one.

It is a rule of thumb though, not an answer. It ignores whether you are staying in the house, whether the ductwork can support a modern system, and whether the fault is a wear part or a major component. Use it to sanity check a decision, not to make it.

Signs a system is near the end

  • It struggles to hold the temperature you set on the hottest afternoons, even though it is clean and the filter is new
  • Rooms that used to be comfortable no longer are
  • It runs almost continuously in July and August rather than cycling
  • It has needed refrigerant added more than once, which means it has a leak
  • Something has changed in how it sounds, particularly at start up
  • Your electricity use has climbed with no other change in the household

A repair quote is not a replacement pitch. If your system only needs a repair, we will tell you that. We would rather fix what you have and be the people you call in five years than sell you something you did not need today.

If you do replace, what changes

Two things are worth knowing before you start looking at equipment. The first is that a replacement is an opportunity to correct sizing. Many systems in older El Paso homes were sized by rule of thumb rather than a load calculation, and an oversized system cools the air quickly without running long enough to remove humidity or reach the far rooms.

The second is that the ductwork matters as much as the box outside. If the house was built for an evaporative cooler and later converted, the ducts may be undersized for refrigerated air. Putting a larger, more efficient system onto restrictive ductwork does not deliver what the label promises. Our refrigerated air conversion page covers how that is assessed.

If your home has rooms with no ductwork at all, a ductless mini-split is often a better answer than extending ducts into them.

Questions about replacing an air conditioner

What is the average lifespan of a home air conditioner?

Commonly quoted as fifteen to twenty years, though that assumes average use. In El Paso, where a system may run most of the day for several months, the working life sits at the lower end of that range. Maintenance makes a real difference to where in the range a particular system lands.

What is the most expensive repair on an air conditioner?

The compressor, followed by the evaporator or condenser coil. These are the repairs where the age of the system matters most, because on an older unit the cost approaches a meaningful share of replacing it.

What are the signs of compressor failure?

Hard starting or a noticeable clunk at start up, the breaker tripping when the system starts, warm air from the vents while the outdoor fan runs, or a hum from the outdoor unit with no fan movement. Any of these is worth switching the system off and calling about.

Can I still get refrigerant for an R-22 system?

Yes, but only reclaimed or previously produced supply, since new production was phased out. It is available and it is legal to use, but the cost reflects the scarcity, which is what makes a large R-22 repair a genuine decision point rather than a routine one.

Will a new system actually lower my bills?

A newer system will generally use less electricity for the same cooling, and how much depends on what you have now, how the house is built and how the ducts perform. We will not quote you a percentage, because the honest answer depends on your house and anyone promising a specific figure before seeing it is guessing.
