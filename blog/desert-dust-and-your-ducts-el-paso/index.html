How often should air ducts be cleaned in El Paso?

The usual national guidance is every three to five years. In El Paso the shorter end of that range is the more realistic one, because the air here carries far more fine dust and systems run for more hours of the year. The better answer is not a fixed interval at all: have the ducts looked at when you notice dust settling quickly after cleaning, visible debris around the registers, or airflow that has dropped without an obvious cause.

Most duct cleaning advice is written for humid climates where the concern is moisture and mould. Here the issue is different. It is fine mineral dust, blown in during spring winds, tracked in on shoes, and pulled into the return every time the system runs.

That dust does not stay in the ducts. It circulates, settles on the coil, loads the filter, and comes back out of the supply vents. Once there is enough of it, it starts to affect how well the system moves air.

Signs your ducts need attention

  • You dust the house and it looks like you did not, two days later
  • Visible dust or fibres around the supply registers, or streaking on the ceiling above them
  • Airflow from the vents has weakened with a clean filter fitted
  • The filter is grey well before the interval on the packaging
  • Rooms furthest from the air handler have become noticeably harder to keep comfortable
  • You have finished a remodel, or the house sat empty through a windy spring

What duct cleaning does, and what it does not

It removes accumulated dust and debris from the duct runs, the registers and the return, so the system moves air the way it was designed to and less of what is in the ducts ends up back in your rooms. Where a lot has built up, the change in airflow is noticeable.

It is worth being straight about the limits. The United States Environmental Protection Agency's position is that duct cleaning has not been shown to prevent health problems, and that dust in ducts does not necessarily mean the air in your home is unhealthy. We are not going to tell you it will fix allergies, because the evidence does not support that claim.

It is also not a repair. If your ducts leak, are undersized, or are disconnected somewhere in the attic, cleaning them will not fix any of that. A good cleaning visit should tell you if that is what you actually have.

Ask to see the ducts before and after. We show you, because it is the only way for you to judge whether the work was worth it rather than taking our word for it.

Cleaning and air quality go together

Cleaning is a reset, not a maintenance plan. What keeps ducts clean afterwards is what happens at the filter and at the return. A filter that suits your system and gets changed on a schedule that matches this climate does more over a year than a cleaning does.

If dust is a persistent problem in your house, the more useful conversation is usually about filtration and sealing rather than repeat cleanings. Our indoor air quality page covers the options, and duct cleaning explains how we do the work itself.

Questions about duct cleaning

Does duct cleaning actually improve airflow?

Where there is significant buildup, yes, and it is measurable at the registers. Where the ducts are already reasonably clean, the honest answer is that you will not notice much, which is why we would rather look first than sell you a cleaning you do not need.

Does duct cleaning help with allergies?

We will not claim that it does. The EPA's guidance is that duct cleaning has not been shown to prevent health problems. If allergies are the reason you are asking, filtration and sealing are usually the more productive route.

How long does it take?

Most single family homes are a few hours, depending on the size of the system and how many runs there are. We will give you a realistic idea when we see the layout.

Is duct cleaning the same as duct sealing?

No, and they solve different problems. Cleaning removes what is inside the ducts. Sealing closes the gaps and disconnections that let conditioned air escape into the attic. A house can need one, the other, or both.

Should I clean the ducts after a remodel?

Usually yes. Construction produces fine drywall dust that gets everywhere, and it is one of the clearer cases where cleaning is worth doing rather than a matter of judgement.
