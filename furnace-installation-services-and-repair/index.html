Why El Paso furnaces fail in the first cold week

Because they have not run since March. A furnace sits idle through a long desert summer, then gets asked for full output on the first cold night, and the weak component gives out.

El Paso heating season is short and sharp. That is exactly what makes it hard on equipment: eight months of dust and stillness, then a hard start. The parts that usually fail are the igniter, the flame sensor, the blower motor capacitor and the thermostat, and every one of them is cheaper to find in October than to discover at midnight in December.

It is also the reason heating problems arrive in a rush. When the first genuine cold snap hits, every HVAC company in the city gets the same phone calls on the same evening.

Signs your heating needs attention before winter

  • The system smells of burning dust for more than the first few minutes of the season
  • Short cycling, where the furnace turns on and off repeatedly
  • The blower runs but the air is not warm
  • Yellow rather than blue burner flame
  • Banging, rattling or squealing on start-up
  • Rooms that used to heat evenly and no longer do
  • The carbon monoxide alarm has sounded, which means leave the house and call us from outside

Heating questions we get asked

Why does my furnace smell like burning when I first turn it on?

Dust that settled on the heat exchanger over the summer burns off the first time the furnace runs. That smell should fade within an hour or so of running. If it persists, or smells electrical or like burning plastic, shut the system off and call us.

How often should a furnace be serviced in El Paso?

Once a year, ideally in early October before the heating season starts. In a desert climate the bigger enemy is dust and long idle periods rather than heavy use, so an annual check catches what the summer left behind.

My heater will not turn on at all. What should I check first?

Check that the thermostat is set to heat and above room temperature, that the furnace switch and breaker are on, and that the filter is not completely blocked. If all three are fine, the fault is usually the igniter or the flame sensor and needs a technician.
